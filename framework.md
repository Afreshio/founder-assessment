# Combined framework: Working Genius × Amazon Leadership Principles

The six Working Genius types (Wonder, Invention, Discernment, Galvanizing,
Enablement, Tenacity) describe the kinds of work that give a person energy.
Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles describe behaviors a leader is expected to
show. This framework uses the six geniuses as the structure and the
principles as the observable behaviors inside each one.

Each principle has one primary home so that scores roll up cleanly. Where a
principle clearly spans two geniuses, the second is listed as a secondary.

## The map

| Phase | Genius | What it does | Primary principles | Secondary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ideation | **Wonder** | Asks whether things could be better; spots the unmet need | Customer Obsession · Learn and Be Curious · Think Big | Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility |
| Ideation | **Invention** | Creates the novel solution | Invent and Simplify · Frugality | Think Big |
| Activation | **Discernment** | Judges whether the idea is right | Are Right, A Lot · Dive Deep · Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | Insist on the Highest Standards · Customer Obsession |
| Activation | **Galvanizing** | Rallies people to act | Bias for Action · Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit | Think Big |
| Implementation | **Enablement** | Gives people the support they need | Earn Trust · Hire and Develop the Best · Strive to be Earth's Best Employer | — |
| Implementation | **Tenacity** | Pushes the work over the line | Deliver Results · Ownership · Insist on the Highest Standards | Bias for Action |

## Why each principle sits where it does

**Wonder**
- *Customer Obsession*: starts from the customer and works backwards, which is the question "what do they need that they don't have?"
- *Learn and Be Curious*: the closest match in the set; curiosity about possibilities.
- *Think Big*: seeing a larger potential than others see. The "communicate a bold direction" half belongs to Galvanizing.

**Invention**
- *Invent and Simplify*: the direct match. Simplifying is itself an inventive act.
- *Frugality*: Amazon defines it as constraints that "breed resourcefulness, self-sufficiency, and invention", so it is scored here as inventing under constraint, not as cost control.

**Discernment**
- *Are Right, A Lot*: good judgment and instincts, plus seeking out views that disconfirm your own.
- *Dive Deep*: being skeptical when the metrics and the anecdotes disagree.
- *Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility*: weighing the second-order effects of a decision.

**Galvanizing**
- *Bias for Action*: getting people moving on reversible decisions without waiting for certainty.
- *Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit*: challenging openly, then rallying fully behind the decision.

**Enablement**
- *Earn Trust*: listening, candor, and respect, which are what make help welcome.
- *Hire and Develop the Best*: coaching and raising the bar for others.
- *Strive to be Earth's Best Employer*: building an environment where others can succeed.

**Tenacity**
- *Deliver Results*: the direct match; finishing despite setbacks.
- *Ownership*: never "that's not my job"; following through over the long term.
- *Insist on the Highest Standards*: defects don't get sent down the line. The "recognizing what good looks like" half belongs to Discernment.

## Purpose: matching executives to companies

The framework is for executives choosing where to work. It compares two
profiles:

- **The executive's profile**: two geniuses, two competencies, and two
  frustrations, from Working Genius.
- **The company's profile**: which geniuses its culture rewards, read from
  the behaviors it says it expects of leaders.

## Company profiles

A company's behavior list shows which kinds of work it prizes. Counting how
many of its behaviors fall under each genius gives a rough profile.

| Company | Behavior set | Leans toward | Light on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon | 16 Leadership Principles | Tenacity, Discernment | none; all six are covered |
| Netflix | Valued behaviors (culture memo) | Galvanizing, Enablement | Tenacity |
| Google | Project Oxygen manager behaviors | Enablement | Wonder, Invention |
| Spotify | Five company values | Invention, Enablement | Discernment, Tenacity |

Netflix's valued behaviors map as: Curiosity (Wonder), Creativity
(Invention), Judgment (Discernment), Courage and Candor (Galvanizing),
Selflessness and Inclusion (Enablement), Resilience (Tenacity).

Google's manager behaviors map as: strong decision maker and technical
skills (Discernment); clear vision and good communicator (Galvanizing); good
coach, empowers the team, inclusive environment, supports career
development, and collaborates across the company (Enablement); productive
and results-oriented (Tenacity).

Spotify's values map as: Innovative (Invention); Playful (Invention, with
Wonder as a secondary); Passionate (Galvanizing, with Tenacity as a
secondary); Collaborative and Sincere (Enablement). Five broad values give
a coarser profile than a longer behavior list does.

The Netflix, Google, and Spotify lists were written from memory and need checking
against the current published versions.

## Three kinds of fit

An executive can fit a company in three different ways, and they can point
in different directions.

1. **Culture fit**: the company rewards the work that gives the executive
   energy. Their geniuses match what the company leans toward.
2. **Gap fit**: the leadership team is missing the executive's genius. They
   are needed because they are different, not because they are similar.
3. **Role fit**: the day-to-day work of the specific role draws on their
   geniuses and competencies, not their frustrations.

Frustrations work as the warning. If the behaviors a company weights most
heavily fall under an executive's frustrations, they can succeed there only
at a steady personal cost.

| | Role uses their geniuses | Role uses their frustrations |
|---|---|---|
| **Culture rewards their geniuses** | Best fit | Right company, wrong role |
| **Culture rewards their frustrations** | Valued specialist, but swimming upstream | Avoid |

Example: an executive with Galvanizing and Enablement as geniuses and
Tenacity and Invention as frustrations is a culture fit for a Netflix-style
company and a poor one for an Amazon-style company, where Deliver Results,
Ownership, and Invent and Simplify are central.

## Limits

- Stated behaviors are what a company says it values. The lived culture can
  differ, so each company profile needs evidence from interviews,
  references, and people who have worked there.
- Counting behaviors per genius is a rough weighting. A single principle a
  company enforces hard can outweigh three it rarely mentions.
- Working Genius measures energy, not skill. The match predicts how
  sustainable a job will be, not how well the executive will perform in it.

## Open choices

- Think Big, Insist on the Highest Standards, and Customer Obsession each
  have a defensible second home. If single-homing distorts the scores, split
  their weight 50/50 across the two geniuses.
- Galvanizing and Invention have two primary principles each and the others
  have three, so genius scores should be averaged, not summed.
- Company profiles could come from stated behaviors alone or be adjusted
  with evidence of the lived culture. The second is more accurate and more
  work.
